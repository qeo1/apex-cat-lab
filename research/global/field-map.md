# Global research field map: first discovery pass

Snapshot: 2026-10-31. This is a research capability and source-discovery record, not a claim that the listed subjects have all been comprehensively investigated. Bibliographic links and evidence limits are maintained in `SOURCE_LEDGER.md` (GLOB-01–GLOB-10).

| Field | Live source reviewed | Problem-design affordance | CAT relevance boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics / discrete mathematics | MIT 18.200 lecture map | Pigeonhole arguments, inclusion-exclusion, generating functions, modular arithmetic, flow and graph representations | Use only if a compact item can be solved with stated prerequisites; unfamiliar terminology alone is not value. |
| Probability | MIT 18.05 / 6.041 | Sample-space design, conditioning, base rates, expectation and count-vs-probability traps | Keep probability assumptions explicit; do not presume the exam's exact syllabus without official material. |
| Operations research | MIT 18.200 linear programming, duality, zero-sum games, max-flow/min-cut modules | Resource allocation, bottlenecks, feasible region and invariants; strong source of DILR structures | Simplify to transparent finite constraints; avoid requiring university optimisation algorithms. |
| Philosophy | Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy on thought experiments and argument | Counterexample tests, hidden assumptions, analogical limits, premise/conclusion separation | Translate into short readable arguments; do not test philosophical name recall. |
| Economics | Nobel Prize Committee materials on repeated games and common-pool governance | Incentives, strategic response, mechanism effects and collective-action trade-offs | Distinguish stipulated model outcomes from claims about real-world behaviour. |
| Statistics | MIT 18.05 | Distinguish sample, estimator, uncertainty, correlation/independence, and interpretation limits | Make data and definitions self-contained; do not make unsupported causal claims. |
| Analytical writing | Purdue OWL Toulmin and argument organization | Claim–grounds–warrant, qualifiers, counterclaim/rebuttal and evidence scope | Supports VARC passage and distractor logic; never use “writing advice” as empirical evidence of a CAT pattern. |

## Pipeline rule emerging from the source review

For each future original question, keep four separate records: (1) a source note naming a specific concept or reasoning move, (2) a CAT benchmark fingerprint and comparison, (3) a novel design transformation that changes the actual structure, and (4) independent solution/uniqueness review. The source table alone is not provenance for an individual question. No pilot question is admitted yet.
